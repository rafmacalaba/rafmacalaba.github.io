Most of my writing ends up being read by people who have not lived inside the
field with me. That's fine. The challenge is that I can usually feel what I am
trying to say before I can say it, and the natural move is to write the version
I would want to read as an expert.

That version is wrong for the audience. It hides the load-bearing assumptions
behind vocabulary that the reader does not share.

## A working rule

One technical term per paragraph. Each term is the topic of the sentence it
appears in, not buried mid-clause. If you find yourself using a term that the
sentence could do without, cut it.

## A test

After drafting, read the piece aloud and ask: *could a smart colleague from a
different field follow the argument?* If the answer is no, the piece is not yet
about its subject. It is about the writer's relationship to the subject.
