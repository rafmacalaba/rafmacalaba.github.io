How often do World Bank projects use geospatial data?

Evidence from 2,261 projects, read through 3,498 Project Appraisal and Project Information Documents

In 6 in 10 World Bank projects that use data (1,090 of 1,823, 60%), teams draw on geospatial data, mostly at the level of regions and districts. Under the strictest checks the share is 50%. Where a PAD was reviewed: 71% (771 of 1,092). 791 of these projects have only one passage read, so every share here is a floor. How reliable is this?

Used in every sector and region, and under-counted

We reviewed sampled passages, not whole documents. When we read one passage of a project, 41% show geospatial data; when we read five or more, 88% do. Projects with two or more documents reviewed reach 75%, against 48% with one document. Read in full, more projects would likely count.

Larger, data-heavy projects also had more passages reviewed, so part of the rise reflects the projects themselves.

So whatGeospatial data is already part of routine project preparation, not a specialist add-on.

How this was measured

Out of which projects?

Those that use data (the default) counts projects with at least one data mention judged used. All projects adds the 438 projects that mention data only as plans, bare figures or sources not relied on. Every one of the 2,261 projects in the collection has at least one reviewed passage, so this is every project, not a sample of them.

Projects or documents?

A project usually has more than one PAD or PID. Projects count a project once: it uses geospatial data if any of its reviewed documents does. Documents count each PAD or PID on its own. The project view is the reporting unit, because region, sector and approval year belong to the project. The project share is higher because a project has more text in which geospatial data can appear. The same document sometimes arrives from two collections under two ids; it is counted once, by its World Bank document id, and a passage read twice counts once. 4,708 confirmed document ids are 4,679 distinct documents.

What counts as geospatial data?

Data the document ties to a place: an exact location (a school, a gauge, a village), a named area (a region, district, camp or an urban/rural split; admin level 1 or 2 is enough), or a figure derived from maps or satellite imagery. Coordinates are not required. A topic such as agriculture or displacement does not count on its own.

What counts as using data?

Each data mention was reviewed for use: does the passage rely on existing data, or only name it, plan to collect it, or cite a bare figure? Only mentions judged as used count. Most judgements are reviewer proposals; where a senior reviewer ruled, that ruling applies.

Why not count datasets?

The same dataset is named many ways (“DHS”, “2016 UDHS”, “Demographic and Health Survey”), so counts of distinct datasets are unreliable. Whether a project or document uses geospatial data is a clear yes or no. Source names are merged across spellings for display only.

How reliable are these figures?

Every number is counted from the review records; none is typed in. An AI-assisted reviewer labelled each data mention against a written rubric and quoted the passage words behind each place label. People have not yet checked the place labels, and 0 of 7,663 judgements that data was used carry a senior reviewer's ruling.

Under stricter rules the share falls. For projects that use data it is 60% without uncertain labels and 50% when the quoted evidence must also match word for word; for documents, 52% without uncertain labels and 42% when the quoted evidence must also match word for word. The word-for-word rule is harsh, because quotes taken from tables often reorder the cells. A defensible statement is between 50% and 60% of projects. A person should check a random sample of projects before the figure is cited externally.

Coverage and limits

2,261 of 2,261 projects had passages reviewed, through 3,498 of their 4,679 distinct PADs and PIDs. 1,823 of the 1,823 data-using projects were approved in 2020 or later, so this describes recent practice and does not show a trend. Sector and region come from the World Bank Projects API; sectors and regions with fewer than 40 projects or documents are not shown. Kinds of data are grouped from source names; their "tied to a place" share counts individual uses. Review sets used: PAD/PID review — original sample (4,932 passages); PAD/PID review — 500-passage addition (500 passages). A PAD/PID counts only when the World Bank documents catalogue confirms it: the document exists, has the stated type and belongs to the stated project. The same document arriving from two collections is counted once, by its World Bank document id.
