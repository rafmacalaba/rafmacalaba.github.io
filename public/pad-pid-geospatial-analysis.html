How often do World Bank projects use geospatial data?

Evidence from 1,204 projects, read through 1,591 Project Appraisal and Project Information Documents

Count by

In over two-thirds of World Bank projects that use data (710 of 1,018, 70%), teams draw on geospatial data, mostly at the level of regions and districts. Under the strictest checks the share is 57%. How reliable is this?

  1. 1,348projects with a PAD or PID in the collection
  2. 1,204had passages reviewed
  3. 1,018use data in those passages
  4. 710use geospatial data

Mainstream in every sector and region, and under-counted

We reviewed sampled passages, not whole documents. When we read one passage of a project, 52% show geospatial data; when we read five or more, 89% do. Projects with two or more documents reviewed reach 85%, against 64% with one document. Read in full, more projects would likely count.

Larger, data-heavy projects also had more passages reviewed, so part of the rise reflects the projects themselves.

So whatGeospatial data is already part of routine project preparation, not a specialist add-on.

What each level looks like in a document

Censuses and sector records carry the geography; big surveys are quoted as national totals

Uses of population censuses (65%), infrastructure records (62%) and climate and weather data (70%) are usually tied to a place.

Enterprise surveys (9%), labour force surveys (14%) and Demographic and Health Surveys (21%) are cited in many projects, but mostly as national figures, even though their data usually holds regional or district detail.

Maps, GIS and satellite data are almost always tied to a place (97%), but appear in fewer than 7 in 100 projects.

How this was measured

Projects or documents?

A project usually has more than one PAD or PID. Projects count a project once: it uses geospatial data if any of its reviewed documents does. Documents count each PAD or PID on its own. The project view is the reporting unit, because region, sector and approval year belong to the project. The project share is higher because a project has more text in which geospatial data can appear.

What counts as geospatial data?

Data the document ties to a place: an exact location (a school, a gauge, a village), a named area (a region, district, camp or an urban/rural split; admin level 1 or 2 is enough), or a figure derived from maps or satellite imagery. Coordinates are not required. A topic such as agriculture or displacement does not count on its own.

What counts as using data?

Each data mention was reviewed for use: does the passage rely on existing data, or only name it, plan to collect it, or cite a bare figure? Only mentions judged as used count. Most judgements are reviewer proposals; where a senior reviewer ruled, that ruling applies.

Why not count datasets?

The same dataset is named many ways (“DHS”, “2016 UDHS”, “Demographic and Health Survey”), so counts of distinct datasets are unreliable. Whether a project or document uses geospatial data is a clear yes or no. Source names are merged across spellings for display only.

How reliable are these figures?

Every number is counted from the review records; none is typed in. An AI-assisted reviewer labelled each data mention against a written rubric and quoted the passage words behind each place label. People have not yet checked the place labels, and 8 of 5,119 judgements that data was used carry a senior reviewer's ruling.

Under stricter rules the share falls. For projects it is 68% without uncertain labels and 57% when the quoted evidence must also match word for word; for documents, 64% without uncertain labels and 53% when the quoted evidence must also match word for word. The word-for-word rule is harsh, because quotes taken from tables often reorder the cells. A defensible statement is between 57% and 70% of projects. A person should check a random sample of projects before the figure is cited externally.

Coverage and limits

1,204 of 1,348 projects had passages reviewed, through 1,591 of their 1,918 PADs and PIDs. 880 of the 1,018 data-using projects were approved in 2020 or later, so this describes recent practice and does not show a trend. Sector and region come from the World Bank Projects API; sectors and regions with fewer than 40 projects or documents are not shown. Kinds of data are grouped from source names; their "tied to a place" share counts individual uses. Reviews combined: Council-reviewed sample (73 passages); Refugee-project documents (588 passages); Human-reviewed sample (1,034 passages); PAD/PID review — original sample (2,203 passages); PAD/PID review — 500-passage addition (314 passages).
