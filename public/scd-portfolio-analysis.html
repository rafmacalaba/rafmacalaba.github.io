How this was computed

Matching seriesA series is one indicator in a database, with its own code (e.g. SI.POV.NAHC, the poverty rate at national poverty lines). Matched on names only, not on definitions, units or years, so true coverage is at least this high. WDI is counted separately from the other Data360 databases. Each SCD–indicator pair counts once.

How the analysis was done, in short. Every number in the brief and in these tables comes from the steps below.

Data

Which SCDs are covered?

All 164 Systematic Country Diagnostics disclosed in the World Bank Documents & Reports API, 2014–2024, for 112 countries: 126 full SCDs, 37 SCD updates and 1 streamlined CD. It is the whole disclosed portfolio, not a sample. Region and income group come from the World Bank country classification.

Where do the data come from?

From the SCD PDFs themselves (10,185 charts, tables, boxes and maps), compared with the World Bank’s own catalogs: the WDI catalog (1,498 series, World Bank API), the Data360 catalog (9,850 indicators in 171 databases), the Data360 list of 5 themes and 24 topics, and the SCD ‘useful databases’ list (55 databases). Nothing was coded by hand.

Is WDI part of Data360?

Yes. WDI is one of Data360’s 171 databases (1,434 of the 1,498 WDI series are in it). This page always counts WDI separately from the other 170 Data360 databases (Enterprise Surveys, WGI, Findex, WEF, IMF WEO…).

Indicators

How were the indicators found?

Each chart and table was located from its caption and source line. GLiNER2, an open AI model that picks names out of text, extracted the indicators from captions, legends and notes. A language model (Claude, through the Bank’s mAIFactory) only cleaned up the names; it could merge fragments of one indicator but not add new ones. Rules read the source line, time span and comparisons. Numbers quoted only in the narrative text are not counted.

How were indicator names harmonized?

Names were normalized into 4,124 indicators, then a rule merged variants that differ only by poverty line, price basis, unit, rural/urban or female/male split, giving 4,095 indicators (e.g. all poverty-line versions of the poverty rate count as one). Differently worded versions of one measure are not always merged, so the count of one-SCD indicators is an upper bound.

What is an ‘indicator use’?

One indicator shown in one SCD, counted once however many charts show it. There are 10,706 uses. Shares such as ‘53% are in Data360’ are shares of uses.

What are the ‘standard’ indicators?

The 15 indicators used by at least 25% of all SCDs and by at least 10% of SCDs in every region and every income group. The cut-offs are ours. Other cut-offs (share of all SCDs / minimum in every group) give: 20%/5% → 30 indicators, 25%/10% → 15 indicators, 30%/10% → 13 indicators, 25%/15% → 10 indicators, 50%/20% → 4 indicators. All 15 were checked by hand against WDI.

Topics

How were themes and topics tagged?

Each indicator gets one of the 24 official Data360 topics, assigned by a language model from the indicator’s name; the theme follows from the topic (5 themes: Prosperity, People, Planet, Infrastructure, Digital). Indicators that fit no topic (e.g. population structure, conflict) are left outside the taxonomy (4% of uses). As a cross-check, where the indicator has a Data360 series, the catalog’s own topic is shown; the two agree for 67% of the 1,222 indicators that have both. The catalog often files a series by platform rather than subject (e.g. employment by sector under Gender).

How was Growth and Jobs split into groups?

By words in the indicator name, first matching rule wins: growth breakdowns (contribution, decomposition), productivity, jobs and wages, then output, income and spending; anything else is ‘Other’. The rule is ours and is printed under the table.

Data360

How do we know whether an indicator is in Data360?

Each indicator name was matched to the WDI catalog (by code or name) and to the Data360 catalog by name, with a language model accepting or rejecting candidate matches; WDI is preferred when both match. Only names were compared, not definitions, units or the countries and years covered, so the shares are a lower bound.

How was the credited source read?

From each chart’s source and note lines. A source line naming several sources counts once, in priority order: WDI, another Data360 database, another international source, national sources, own analysis or studies. ‘World Bank only’ means the line names the World Bank but no World Bank database (e.g. ‘Source: World Bank.’).

Datasets

How were datasets found and harmonized?

A data-mention model (GLiNER, fine-tuned: rafmacalaba/gliner_agents_v3) read the full text and found 11,371 mentions of named datasets. Names were normalized (years, brackets, ‘data’/‘database’ removed), then mapped to one name per program (DHS, MICS, LSMS, PISA, WDI, Enterprise Surveys…); generic words such as ‘survey’ or ‘census’ are not datasets. Result: 3,880 datasets; 26 report titles and generic terms excluded. The 47 named by 10 or more SCDs were checked by name against the Data360 databases.

Other

How were data gaps found?

Candidate sentences were found by GLiNER2 and a keyword screen; a language model confirmed each one and labelled the kind of data and the problem (missing, quality, coverage, outdated, comparability). 2,527 gap statements in 159 SCDs.

How were changes over time and differences between groups tested?

Three periods (2014–2017, 2018–2020, 2021–2024). Over time, only full SCDs are compared, because recent SCDs are mostly shorter updates. Indicator use: Fisher exact test, first vs last period; topic shares: Mann-Whitney test. Group differences: Fisher exact test, group vs the rest. All corrected for the number of tests (Benjamini-Hochberg). A change or difference is reported only when the test supports it.

How reliable are the numbers?

Two other extractors were run over the same charts as a cross-check (GLiNER v1 and a language model reading each chart). They rank indicators by how many SCDs use them in a similar order (rank correlation 0.71 and 0.63), and 12 and 13 of the 15 standard indicators are also in at least 20% of SCDs under each. Exact counts per SCD vary by extractor: read counts as approximate, shares and rankings as solid.

What is not covered?

Numbers quoted only in the narrative; indicator definitions (rarely stated in charts); whether a matched series has data for the country and years the SCD used; the Data360 status of datasets named by fewer than 10 SCDs; other data platforms (IMF, ILO, UN, OECD sites) as sources.
